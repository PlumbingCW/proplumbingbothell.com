Bothell plumbing.

Plumbing in Bothell, what is different here

.

Soft water, older pipes and city rules

.

The City of Bothell says its drinking water is soft and comes from Seattle Public Utilities, from the Tolt and Cedar watersheds. Soft water means less scale in heaters and fixtures, but it does not protect old pipes from age, pressure or freezing.

The city grew fast after 1950, so many homes are decades old and may have galvanized steel, older copper or clay and cast iron drains. If you have recurring leaks, repiping is worth a look, and a camera in the sewer line, see sewer inspection, shows what is underground. Our work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56.

Who owns what in a plumbing emergency

.

Bothell and several water districts serve different parts of the city. The City FAQ says homeowners are responsible for repairs beyond the water meter, including the service line. For a leak in the yard, see water service line repair.

Replacing a water heater in Bothell requires a permit, and other repairs may need one too. We tell you what applies before we start. For a failed tank, see water heater replacement.
