First step.

Water under the sink: close the little valves first

.

Look under the sink for the two small oval or round handles on the supply lines. Turn them clockwise until they stop. If the valves do not close or are stuck, shut off the main water supply to the house and call us.

A supply line that fails can spray hot or cold water, and a faucet body that leaks inside the cabinet can ruin a base cabinet quickly. Do not wait for it to dry out.

When a drip is worth fixing today

.

A slow drip wastes little water in a day, but over a month it adds up and it stains the sink. A leak under the sink is more urgent. It can soak the cabinet floor and, over weeks, rot it. If you find a damp cabinet, check the valves and the supply lines first.

Hot water leaks matter a little more because you pay to heat what drips away. A faucet that drips only when the hot side is on usually has a worn hot-side cartridge or seat.

What to tell us when you call

.
  • Kitchen or bathroom faucet, and the brand if you know it.
  • Whether the leak is at the spout, the handle or below the sink.
  • Whether the shutoff valves under the sink still close.
  • How old the faucet is.
  • Whether the drip is hot, cold or both.
Local note.

Faucets and water in Bothell

.

The City of Bothell describes its drinking water as soft, which is easier on faucets than hard water. Faucets still wear out, and old shutoff valves under the sink are a common point of failure in older homes.

If your sink is slow rather than leaking, a clog in the drain could be the issue, so see our drain cleaning page. If your garbage disposal sits under the same sink and is leaking, see garbage disposal repair.

Faucet and supply work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56.
