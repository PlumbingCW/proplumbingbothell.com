Overview.

What changes when you go tankless

.

A tankless heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it. The upside is hot water that does not run out and a small footprint. The tradeoff is that the unit needs enough gas or electric capacity to heat a full flow, and venting that meets code.

That is why the planning visit matters. Many tankless problems come from a gas line that is too small or a vent run that is too long, not from the unit itself.

Gas or electric tankless

.

Gas tankless

.
  • Heats a high flow, suited to whole homes.
  • Needs a properly sized gas line.
  • Needs approved venting and combustion air.
  • Safety first: gas smell means leave and call 911.

Electric tankless

.
  • No venting needed.
  • Needs a high-amp circuit and sometimes a service upgrade.
  • Often used for point of use.
  • Limits on flow in cold incoming water.

Signs your tankless needs service

.
  • An error code on the display.
  • Hot water that cuts in and out.
  • Reduced flow or lukewarm water.
  • Rumbling or ticking noises, often from scale.
  • Water leaking at the unit or its valves.

Sizing a tankless heater for your household

.

A tankless unit is rated by how much hot water it can deliver at once, and that depends on the temperature rise you need. Water arrives cold, and the unit must lift it to your setting. A shower and a dishwasher running together draw more than a single shower.

We add up the fixtures you might run at the same time and choose a unit that covers that load. Buying too small leads to lukewarm water in busy hours, and too large adds cost without a benefit.

Common error codes and what they point to

.

Each brand has its own codes, but the causes are similar: a clogged inlet filter, a blocked vent, a gas supply issue, a scale-restricted heat exchanger or a sensor fault. Writing down the code and the model number helps us diagnose by phone before we arrive.

Do not keep resetting a unit that keeps faulting. Repeated resets can hide a safety problem.

Local note.

Descaling in soft-water Bothell

.

The City of Bothell says its drinking water is soft. That helps tankless heaters, since scale builds slowly. A flush is still part of normal care, because even soft water carries some minerals.

Ask us how often your model should be flushed. For a standard tank unit, see water heater installation. If a tank has failed, water heater replacement covers that.

Tankless gas and venting work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in WAC 51-56 and the unit manufacturer instructions.
