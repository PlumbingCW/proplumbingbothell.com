Preventing the next business emergency

.

Many commercial plumbing emergencies have warning signs. Slow floor drains, a grease trap that has not been serviced and restroom fixtures that run constantly are all early alerts. A short walk-through by a plumber can spot items that need attention before they stop business.

Know where the main shutoff is, post it where staff can find it and keep the number for the plumber handy. In a restaurant, schedule grease trap cleaning on a regular basis and train staff on what goes down the drain.

Local note.

Commercial plumbing around Bothell

.

Bothell has retail and office areas along SR 527 near Canyon Park, businesses in Downtown Bothell and around North Creek, and the campus of UW Bothell and Cascadia College. Each uses water and drains differently.

Commercial buildings often have grease traps, backflow devices and larger water heaters that residential plumbers do not routinely service. Check with the landlord or property manager first about who is responsible for repairs. For a main line blockage, see sewer line cleaning and for kitchen lines see hydro jetting.

Commercial work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in WAC 51-56, which has additional requirements for grease interceptors and backflow protection.
