Overview.

Plan the plumbing before the walls close

.

Plumbing work is easiest and cheapest before drywall and finished floors go in. Drain slope, vent routes and supply lines all have to fit the framing and meet code. Moving a toilet or adding a shower after the fact costs far more than planning it first.

Whether you are adding a bathroom, remodeling a kitchen or replacing fixtures, we start by looking at what is in the walls and what the plan needs.

What we install

.
  • Toilets, sinks, tubs and showers.
  • Kitchen sinks, faucets and disposals.
  • Water heater connections and shutoff valves.
  • New supply lines and drain, waste and vent piping.
  • Laundry hookups and dishwasher connections.
  • Outdoor hose bibs and sprinkler connections.

Choosing fixtures that fit your plan

.

Fixtures come with rough-in dimensions, and the pipes in the wall must match. A wall-hung toilet needs a carrier in the wall, a freestanding tub needs a floor-mounted supply and a vanity sink needs the drain at the correct height. Decide which fixtures you want before the walls are framed.

If you already own the fixtures, bring the spec sheets to the visit. We can check that the plan works before any pipe is cut.

Before we schedule the rough-in

.
  • Floor plan or sketch of the room.
  • Fixture models chosen.
  • Location of the main shutoff and the nearest drain.
  • Permit status confirmed.
  • Contractor schedule for framing and inspection.
  • Access for deliveries and a place for materials.
Local note.

Permits and plumbing in Bothell

.

The City of Bothell Permit Center says plumbing repairs and certain replacements may need a permit. Bothell Municipal Code exempts some work, such as repairing leaks or reinstalling a toilet, but exemptions do not authorize code violations.

New bathrooms, added lines and moved fixtures typically need a permit and an inspection. Washington requires plumbing contractors to be licensed, and the state Department of Labor and Industries has a lookup tool to verify a contractor. We tell you what permit applies before work begins. For the existing pipes, see repiping if the supply lines are old.

New work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56, and inspections check drain slope, venting and testing.
