Kitchen Sink Plumbing Explained: Drain, Trap and Air Gap

.

Under a kitchen sink is a small system of pipes that does more than carry water away. Knowing what each part does helps you spot problems before a cabinet floods.

  • Trap and drain.
  • Dishwasher and disposal.
  • Common problems.

By the Bothell Pro Plumbing team.Published October 24, 2026.7 min read.
Short answer: A kitchen sink has a supply side with hot and cold lines and a drain side with a trap, a tailpipe and a vent. The trap holds water to block sewer gas. Dishwashers and disposals tie into the drain.

The supply side: two lines and two valves

.

Hot and cold water reach the faucet through flexible supply lines. Each has a small shutoff valve under the sink, often called an angle stop. Know where they are. Turning them clockwise stops water to the faucet without shutting off the house.

These valves are cheap and sit unused for years, which means they often seize. If yours will not close, replace them before you need them. A stuck valve in an emergency turns a small leak into a flood.

The drain side: basket, trap and branch

.

Water leaves through the strainer basket into the tailpipe and into a curved section called the P-trap. The trap holds a plug of water that blocks sewer gas from coming up the drain. From the trap, the pipe runs to the wall and joins the branch line.

Most clogs happen here. Grease, food and soap collect in the trap. Unscrewing it over a bucket and cleaning it clears many clogs. If the clog is past the trap, the branch line needs a cable, see drain cleaning.

The dishwasher and disposal connections

.

A dishwasher drains through a hose that connects to the disposal or to the sink tailpipe. To keep dirty water from flowing back, the hose is looped high under the counter or connects through an air gap on the sink deck. Which one is required depends on code, so ask when you remodel.

A garbage disposal sits below the sink basket and discharges into the trap. If the dishwasher backs up into the sink, the disposal or the drain past it is usually partly clogged. A jammed disposal needs a different fix, see garbage disposal repair.

Quick checks for common kitchen sink problems

.
  • Slow drain: clean the trap and strainer first, then run hot water.
  • Gurgling: may be a vent problem, so check whether other fixtures gurgle too.
  • Leak at the trap: tighten the slip nuts by hand and check the washers.
  • Leak from the supply line: close the angle stop and replace the line.
  • Smell: dry trap or a dirty disposal, run water and clean the splash guard.

Why vents matter under the counter

.

A sink drain needs air behind the water. Without a vent, the draining water can pull the water seal out of the trap, and the sink gurgles or smells. The vent usually rises inside the wall and joins a larger vent or exits through the roof.

Some remodels use an air admittance valve under the sink when a conventional vent is hard to reach. Whether one is allowed depends on code, so check before you plan a layout around one.

Layout problems that cause repeat clogs

.

Long horizontal runs, too little slope and sharp turns slow the flow and collect grease. A tailpipe that dips below the trap inlet or a trap installed backward also causes trouble. These faults are common in kitchens that were remodeled without a plumber.

If your kitchen sink clogs again and again, the layout may be the cause, not the food. A plumber can look at the arrangement and a camera can show whether the branch line has a low spot.

Annual under-sink check

.
  • Open both angle stops and close them again to keep them working.
  • Look for green or white crust at joints, which means a slow leak.
  • Feel braided supply lines for bulges or fraying.
  • Run the dishwasher and watch the drain connections.
  • Check the cabinet floor for soft or stained wood.
  • Clean the strainer and disposal splash guard.

Kitchen sinks in Bothell homes

.

Bothell has homes from every decade since the 1950s, and kitchens in the older ones have often been remodeled without a full plumbing update. It is common to find a mix of metal and plastic drain pipe under the sink, with old angle stops that no longer close.

The City Permit Center says some plumbing work needs a permit. Bothell Municipal Code exempts certain repairs, such as stopping a leak or replacing a prefabricated fixture. Moving a sink or adding a dishwasher is a different story. For a new fixture or a remodel, see plumbing installation. We serve Kenmore and nearby towns on our service areas page.

Traps, vents and dishwasher drain connections follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56.
