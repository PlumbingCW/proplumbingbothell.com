Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Bothell, WA

.

A gas tankless heater burns a lot of fuel in a short time, so the gas line, vent and combustion air have to be right. This guide covers the checks that matter, with safety first.

  • Gas line sizing.
  • Venting and air.
  • Safety and permit.

By the Bothell Pro Plumbing team.Published October 24, 2026.8 min read.
Short answer: A gas tankless install needs a gas line sized for the unit, approved venting, combustion air, a condensate drain on condensing models and a permit. If you smell gas at any time, leave the house and call 911 from outside.

Safety comes first

.

Gas appliances need respect. A leak at a connection can cause fire, and a poor vent can let carbon monoxide into the house. Install a carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas and test it. If you smell gas, do not turn on lights or use a phone inside. Leave and call 911 and your gas utility from outside.

Gas connections and venting should be done by a licensed professional and inspected under a permit.

What the install requires

.
  1. 1

    Check gas capacity

    .

    Add up the load of the new unit and your other gas appliances. Compare it to the capacity of the line and meter.

  2. 2

    Choose the vent type

    .

    Condensing units use plastic venting. Non-condensing units use metal venting with different clearances.

  3. 3

    Plan combustion air

    .

    The unit needs air to burn fuel. Some models draw it from outside through a sealed vent.

  4. 4

    Set up the condensate drain

    .

    Condensing units produce acidic water that needs a safe drain, often with a neutralizer.

  5. 5

    Connect water and gas

    .

    Isolation valves on the water side, a shutoff and sediment trap on the gas side.

  6. 6

    Test and inspect

    .

    Check for leaks, verify combustion, and have the permit inspection done.

.

Gas line sizing

.

A tankless unit can draw many times the gas of a tank heater. If the line was sized for a tank, it may be too small. Upsizing can mean new pipe from the meter. Your gas utility can tell you about the meter.

Ask for the size check in writing. An undersized line causes error codes, poor output and nuisance shutdowns.

Venting rules

.

Every unit has limits on vent length, number of elbows and where the vent can terminate. Terminations must be clear of windows, doors and air intakes. A vent that does not follow the manual can fail inspection and create a hazard.

We read the manual and plan the route before any cutting. See tankless water heater for service and flushing after the install.

Combustion air and the room it sits in

.

A gas unit needs a steady supply of air to burn fuel cleanly. In a sealed closet it can starve and run poorly. Direct-vent models pull air from outside through a sealed pipe, which avoids the issue. Open-vent models need louvered doors or air openings that meet code.

Tell the installer if the unit is going in a tight space, and expect a plan for air supply in the written scope.

Why annual service matters on gas units

.

A gas tankless unit has burners, sensors and a heat exchanger that should be checked. Flushing the exchanger removes scale. Cleaning the burner and checking the vent catch problems early. Annual service is also a good time to test the carbon monoxide alarms.

Keep the manual and permit copy together with the service record. They help if you sell the house.

Gas tankless safety checklist

.
  • Carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas.
  • Gas shutoff within reach.
  • Sediment trap on the gas line.
  • Vent terminated clear of openings.
  • Combustion air supply confirmed.
  • Leak test on all gas joints.

Gas tankless installs in Bothell

.

Gas service in the area is provided by Puget Sound Energy in many addresses, but not all homes have natural gas. Confirm service at your address before you plan a gas unit. If you are on electric only, an electric or heat pump unit may be the better path.
