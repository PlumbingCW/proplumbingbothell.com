Tankless Water Heater Leaking? Causes and What to Do

.

Water around a tankless unit can come from a loose fitting or from a failed heat exchanger. The first move is to shut things off. The second is to find the source.

  • Shut off first.
  • Common causes.
  • When to call.
Plumber on call.(425) 845-0257. Call now.
By the Bothell Pro Plumbing team.Published October 24, 2026.7 min read.
Short answer: Shut off the water to the unit and turn off its power or gas. Then look for the source: a loose fitting, a relief valve, a condensate line or the heat exchanger. A leak from the heat exchanger needs a plumber.

What to do the moment you see water

.

These steps protect your floor and the unit.

  1. 1

    Close the water valves

    .

    Use the isolation valves on the cold and hot lines to the unit, or the main valve if they are stuck.

  2. 2

    Turn off power or gas

    .

    Switch off the electric supply. For gas units, close the gas shutoff. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911.

  3. 3

    Dry and look

    .

    Wipe the area and watch where the water reappears. Take a photo for later.

  4. 4

    Call if you cannot find it

    .

    A tankless leak inside the cabinet needs a technician.

The usual suspects

.

The most common leak is at a fitting. Connections at the isolation valves, the water pipes and the pressure relief line can loosen with vibration and heat cycles. These are usually a quick repair.

The second most common source is the condensate line on condensing units. If the drain is blocked, water backs up and leaks. It is a simple fix when caught early. A leak from the heat exchanger or inside the cabinet is more serious, and the unit may need repair or replacement.

.

Descaling and why it matters

.

Mineral scale builds up inside the heat exchanger and can cause overheating, noise and eventually leaks. A descaling flush with a pump and a mild acid solution removes the buildup. Most manufacturers recommend periodic flushing.

Bothell water is soft, according to the City, so scale builds slowly. A flush is still a good idea. Ask us how often your model needs it, and see tankless water heater for how we handle flushes and repairs.

Repair or replace

.

A leak at a fitting or a valve is a repair. A cracked heat exchanger often costs close to a new unit. If the unit is older and the repair is major, replacing it may make more sense.

We tell you the options after we find the source. If the unit is beyond repair, our water heater installation page explains what a new unit involves.

What a leak can damage before you notice

.

A tankless unit hangs on a wall, often above finished surfaces. A slow drip can run down drywall, soak insulation or rot a garage wall plate before it shows. Look at the wall below the unit and at the floor for stains, swelling or a musty smell.

A leak can also trip the unit into an error code or cause the circuit board to fail if water reaches it. If the control panel acts strangely after a leak, leave the unit off and have it checked.

Preventing the next leak

.

Flush the unit on schedule, keep the inlet filter clean and check the isolation valves for slow weeps. Make sure the condensate drain flows. Insulate exposed outdoor or garage lines before a cold snap. Test the relief valve if the manual calls for it.

A yearly look by a technician catches loose fittings and early scale before they become leaks.

What to tell the technician

.
  • Model and age of the unit.
  • Gas or electric.
  • Where the water appeared and when you first saw it.
  • Any error codes on the display.
  • Whether the unit has been flushed and when.
  • Whether the water is still shut off.
Bothell angle.

Tankless leaks and Bothell homes

.

In Bothell, tankless units often sit in a garage or on an exterior wall. Garage and outdoor units face temperature swings, and a cold snap can freeze a condensate line or an exposed fitting. Check that exterior lines are insulated.

If the unit sits in a closet or crawl space, a slow leak can run unseen for weeks, so check the area around it from time to time. We serve Kenmore and nearby towns listed on our service areas page. If a gas smell ever accompanies a leak, leave and call 911.
