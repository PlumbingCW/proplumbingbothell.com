How to Fix a Running Toilet: A Bothell Homeowner Guide

.

A toilet that runs or hisses after a flush is wasting water. Most running toilets are fixed with a cheap part and ten minutes. Here is how to find the cause and when to call a plumber.

  • Dye test.
  • Flapper and fill valve.
  • When to call.
Plumber on call.(425) 845-0257. Call now.
By the Bothell Pro Plumbing team.Published October 24, 2026.7 min read.
Short answer: Most running toilets leak from the tank into the bowl past a worn flapper, or the fill valve will not shut off. Replace the flapper first, then check the chain and the fill valve. If it still runs, call a plumber.

Find out where the water is going

.

Remove the tank lid and look. If water is flowing into the overflow tube, the fill valve is not shutting off or the float is set too high. If the water level is fine but the tank slowly empties and the toilet refills on its own, the flapper is leaking into the bowl.

A dye test confirms the second case. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and do not flush. If color shows up in the bowl within ten or fifteen minutes, the flapper is leaking.

Fix it in four steps

.
  1. 1

    Turn off the supply

    .

    Close the valve behind the toilet and flush to empty the tank.

  2. 2

    Check the chain

    .

    A chain that is too short holds the flapper open, and one that is too long can get caught under it. Leave a little slack.

  3. 3

    Replace the flapper

    .

    A new flapper of the same type costs little and fits most toilets. Match the size to your flush valve.

  4. 4

    Adjust or replace the fill valve

    .

    Set the float so the water stops about an inch below the overflow tube. If it will not shut off, replace the valve.

What each symptom usually means

.

Why a running toilet matters on the water bill

.

A toilet leak can waste a lot of water without a sound. You may not hear it at all, but your water meter will. If your bill rises and your habits have not changed, a toilet is one of the first things to check.

Turn off every tap, then read the meter or watch its small low-flow indicator. If it moves, water is leaking somewhere after the meter. The dye test finds toilet leaks, and if the toilet is not the cause, see our guide to a hidden leak trace.

When to stop DIY and call

.

Some problems need a plumber. Water at the base of the toilet means a wax ring or supply line problem. A cracked tank or bowl cannot be repaired. A toilet that runs after you replace both the flapper and the fill valve may have a worn flush valve seat.

If the toilet is old and has needed repeated repairs, replacement may cost less than another fix. We can advise on that during a visit to toilet repair.

Parts that wear out and how long they last

.

The flapper is a rubber part that sits in water all day. It can harden, warp or collect mineral film within a few years, and chemical tank tablets speed that up. The fill valve has seals and a float that also wear. These are low-cost parts, so replace them rather than nursing a worn one.

When you buy a flapper, take the old one to the store or note the brand and flush valve size. A flapper that does not match the valve will leak even when new.

A slow leak you cannot hear

.

Some leaks are silent. Water seeps past the flapper into the bowl so slowly that you never hear the toilet refill. The only sign is a higher water bill or a faint ripple in the bowl. Check each toilet with the dye test once a year, especially in a house with older fixtures.

If a toilet fails the dye test and a new flapper does not fix it, the flush valve seat may be pitted. That repair is quick for a plumber and often means replacing the flush valve or the toilet.

Quick checklist before you call

.
  • Dye test done and result noted.
  • Chain length checked for slack.
  • Flapper replaced with the matching type.
  • Float or fill valve adjusted so water stops below the overflow tube.
  • Supply line and base dry after a flush.
  • Meter checked with all taps off.
