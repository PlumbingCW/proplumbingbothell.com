Why jetting.

A line that keeps clogging cannot wait for a fourth snaking

.

Hydro jetting sends a controlled stream of water through the pipe at a pressure a cable cannot match. The nozzle scours grease, scale and soft roots off the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the middle of them.

If you are dealing with a backup right now, call first. A plumber can tell you whether the situation needs a jetter or whether a cable will get you through the night and a jetting visit can follow.

Cable or jet: how they differ

.

Both are good tools. They solve different problems.

Drain cable

.
  • Breaks through a blockage and restores flow.
  • Fast for hair, soap and a single clog.
  • Can leave a coating of grease on the pipe wall.
  • Cuts roots but does not wash the debris away.

Hydro jetting

.
  • Scours the full pipe wall, not just the middle.
  • Flushes debris downstream and out of the line.
  • Suited to grease, scale and packed roots.
  • Needs a clear cleanout and a sound pipe.

Where jetting pays off

.

These are the situations where we reach for the jetter first.

  • Kitchen drains and main lines coated with years of grease, such as restaurants and busy households.
  • Sewer laterals with fine roots that regrow within months of every cable visit.
  • Cast iron lines with heavy scale that has narrowed the pipe.
  • Long runs where a cable struggles to reach or turn.
  • Preventive cleaning after a backup, once a camera has confirmed the pipe is sound.

What a jetting visit looks like

.
  1. 1

    Camera first

    .

    We look at the line before any pressure goes into it. A cracked or collapsed pipe is not a jetting candidate.

  2. 2

    Set up at the cleanout

    .

    The hose enters through a cleanout. We choose a nozzle for the type of buildup we saw.

  3. 3

    Jet the line

    .

    The nozzle pulls itself forward and cleans on the way back, sending debris toward the main.

  4. 4

    Camera again

    .

    A second pass confirms the pipe wall is clean and shows any damage that the buildup was hiding.

Local note.

Roots, soil and old pipe around Bothell

.

Roots are one of the main reasons lines near the Sammamish River and around North Creek and Canyon Park slow down again after a cable visit. Fine root hairs enter at joints and thicken with every wet season.

Jetting washes them out, and a camera check shows the joint they came through. If the joint is damaged, the long fix is sewer line repair, because a jetted pipe with an open joint will fill with roots again.

Any repair a camera finds after jetting is done to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended in Washington under WAC 51-56.
