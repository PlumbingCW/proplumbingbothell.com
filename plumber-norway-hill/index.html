The area.

Where Norway Hill sits

.

The City of Bothell names this area Waynita, Simonds and Norway Hill. It is south of the Sammamish River and west of Interstate 405, and it includes the former Wayne Golf Course, now city parkland.

Bothell's boundaries moved south of the river in the 1950s, when the city began its first large annexations. Today the area has a mix of detached homes, and some of it lies close to the city line with Kenmore.

The former Wayne Golf Course

.

The City of Bothell has owned the 89-acre former golf course since the end of 2017. It paid $3.8 million with help from King County and the nonprofit Forterra. The Sammamish River runs through the property, and deed restrictions bar housing and industrial uses there.

That makes the park a permanent open space beside the neighborhood. For plumbing, the practical point is simple. This is a place where homes sit near a river, trails and mature trees, so yard-side pipes deserve attention.

Yard-side plumbing worth checking at a detached house

.

These are general items, not a claim about any street here.

  • The main shutoff and the water meter box, and whether you can reach both.
  • Outdoor spigots and hose connections before the first freeze.
  • Sewer cleanout covers, and whether you know where yours is.
  • Slow drains that return after a cable visit, which may point to a line problem.
  • Wet spots in a yard that stay wet without rain.

General plumbing note.

Tree roots and clay pipe: what to check

.

Mature trees are common in older neighborhoods across Western Washington, and roots look for water. A drain that clears and then slows again can have several causes, and roots are one of them.

We do not know which pipe material is under any house here. A camera inspection shows the line, including its material and any damage, so you can decide with facts. See hydro jetting if the line is full of roots or grease.
