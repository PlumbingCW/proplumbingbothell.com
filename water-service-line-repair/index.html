What happened.

A wet yard with no rain usually means a service line leak

.

The water service line carries water from the utility meter to your home. It runs underground, often for dozens of feet, and it can leak for weeks before anything shows. A soft patch of lawn, a sinkhole or a sudden drop in pressure are common first signs.

If water is surfacing or flooding, shut off the valve at your meter or at the house. Then call. We can confirm whether the line is the source and what repair options fit.

Repair a section or replace the line

.

Both are real options. The right one depends on the pipe and how much of it is failing.

Repair one section

.
  • One break or joint is the clear cause.
  • The pipe is otherwise sound.
  • Access is easy and the dig is short.
  • The material is not known for repeated failure.

Replace the line

.
  • Repeated leaks along the same line.
  • Old galvanized, lead or brittle plastic pipe.
  • Low pressure from corrosion inside the pipe.
  • Digging once is more practical than digging again.

Telling a service line leak from an indoor leak

.

Close every fixture and check the meter. If it still moves, water is escaping somewhere. A damp yard points to the buried line. A stain on a ceiling or a warm spot on a slab points inside, and leak detection finds it without opening walls.

If a pipe has already burst indoors, burst pipe repair is the faster page. If the line has also clogged on the sewer side, see sewer line repair.

Signs the line is aging

.

A service line that is old can show warning signs before it fails. Reduced pressure, a meter that creeps when the house is quiet and damp spots in the yard are common. In older homes, the line may be galvanized or another material that corrodes inside and narrows the flow.

If your line is old and you are already digging for a repair, replacing a longer section at the same time can save a second excavation. We explain the options after we see the line.

Local note.

Who handles what between the meter and your house

.

The City of Bothell serves about 8,500 households and businesses with water purchased from Seattle Public Utilities, and four water districts serve parts of the city. The City FAQ says homeowners are responsible for all repairs beyond the water meter, including the service line to the home.

The City report notes that lead in drinking water comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing, and that homes with older lines should flush the tap after the water has sat. If your line is old, replacement is worth discussing. Call 811 at least two business days before any digging so buried utilities are marked.

Service line work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in WAC 51-56. Washington law also requires you to call 811 at least two business days before digging (RCW 19.122).
