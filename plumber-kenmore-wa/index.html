Before we arrive: what to do in Kenmore tonight

.

These steps limit damage while you wait. They apply to any home in Kenmore.

  1. Find the main shutoff.It is usually where the water line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Stop using water.If a drain is backing up, do not run sinks, showers or appliances.
  3. Kill power near water.If water is near outlets or appliances, turn off the breaker for that area. Do not stand in water.
  4. Smell gas? Leave.If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 and your gas utility from outside.
  5. Move valuables.Lift rugs, electronics and papers out of the wet area. Take photos for insurance.
  6. Keep your phone handy.We may call back with questions and to give you directions on what to expect.
Local history, practical result.

Kenmore homes and the plumbing they have

.

Kenmore was incorporated on August 31, 1998, which makes it the newest city in King County, but its housing is much older than the city. The Red Brick Highway, which opened in 1913, connected the area north of Lake Washington, and small tracts platted after World War I filled in north of the highway.

That history means many Kenmore houses are decades old and may still have original supply lines and sewer laterals. Galvanized steel, older copper and clay or cast iron drains are all possible. If your house is one of them, expect pinhole leaks, rusty water and roots in the lateral. A camera inspection shows what is in the line before you commit to a repair, see sewer inspection.

Who to call.

Utilities that serve Kenmore

.

The City of Kenmore names Northshore Utility District as the provider for water and sewer, Puget Sound Energy for electric and natural gas, and Republic Services for garbage. That matters in an emergency.

If water is flooding and you cannot find your shutoff, or if the problem is in the water main or the public sewer, the utility is the right call for the public side. For the pipe on your own lot and everything inside your house, call us. Kenmore is served by a different utility than the City of Bothell. Our work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56.
