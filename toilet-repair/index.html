Common toilet problems and what they mean

.

Constant running

.

A worn flapper or fill valve lets water leak into the bowl. A running toilet can waste a lot of water over a day.

Weak flush

.

Clogged jets under the rim, a low water level or a partial blockage.

Rocking or leaking at the base

.

A failed wax ring or loose bolts let water seep onto the floor.

Repeated clogs

.

A partial blockage in the trap or drain, or a damaged line.

Replacing a toilet that keeps failing

.

If your toilet has needed several repairs, clogs often or is cracked, a new one may be the better choice. Modern toilets flush well with less water. They also have standard parts that are easy to replace. When we replace a toilet we also check the flange, the supply valve and the floor for damage.

A leaking toilet over time can rot the subfloor around the flange. If the floor feels soft near the base, tell us so we can check before we set the new one.

What to check before calling about a toilet

.
  • Whether the problem is only the toilet or other fixtures too.
  • Whether water is on the floor and where it appears.
  • Whether the supply valve still closes.
  • How old the toilet is.
  • Whether anything unusual went down it.
  • Whether the tank refills normally.
Local note.

Toilet clogs and Bothell lines

.

Toilets in older Bothell homes sometimes share a branch with other fixtures, and a slow toilet can point to a line problem. If a bathroom sink and the tub also drain slowly, the clog is deeper than the toilet.

Do not flush wipes, even the ones sold as flushable. If a toilet drains slowly but is not fully blocked, read our guide on drain cleaning to see when the line is the problem. A running toilet is one of the most common causes of a high water bill, so check it before you look for a hidden leak.

Toilet installs and repairs follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56.
